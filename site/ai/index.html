How Folkweb uses AI

Folkweb isn’t against AI. It’s for people. We want AI that humanizes the web: AI that helps you find, understand and connect with other people’s work, on your terms. Here is exactly where AI is and isn’t involved today.

In the extension today: no AI

  • Folkweb currently contains no AI models and sends nothing to AI services.
  • It lets you choose which AI features you see on other sites: AI panels and summaries can be hidden or shown, one setting each.
  • It never uses AI to judge whether something was made by a person. It shows evidence (human.json, Content Credentials, lists you chose) and says “unknown” when there is none.

Exploring next: optional AI that works for you

We’re exploring helpers that use AI to bring you closer to people: plain-language headlines instead of clickbait, short summaries that point to the original author, translation for small personal sites, and help finding the person behind an article. If we build them, they follow these rules:

  • Off until you turn them on, one by one.
  • Clearly labelled as AI, with the original always one click away.
  • Running on your own device where possible. Your browsing is never sent to us.
  • Never used to score or guess whether something was made by a person.

In making Folkweb: as a helper, with a person in charge

  • AI coding assistants help write and review code, tests, documentation and filter fixes.
  • They work under written rules and automatic guards. They can’t publish releases, change permissions or edit lists without a person’s approval.
  • A person reviews and approves every change before it ships.

On this website

The text on folkweb.org was drafted with an AI assistant, then reviewed and approved by the maintainer. If that changes, this page will say so.

AI crawlers

AI assistants that read this site to answer someone’s question, and link back to it, are welcome. We ask crawlers that only collect pages for AI training not to use this site (see robots.txt). Not every crawler respects that.
